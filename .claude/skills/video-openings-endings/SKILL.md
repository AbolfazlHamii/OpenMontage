# Openings and endings

The opening decides whether anyone watches; the ending decides whether they watch the next one. Both are
written first and rewritten last. Evidence grades as in `skills/creative/narration-craft.md`: **A** platform
documentation, **B** research, **C** practice of channels that work, **D** our own thresholds.

## Part 1: the opening

### What each window must do

| Window | Job | Fails when |
|---|---|---|
| 0-3 s | Stop the scroll: the first words already hold a danger, a number or "you". Picture moving. | Logo, "hi", a title, weather, a slow establishing shot (**C/D**) |
| 3-10 s | Make it worse and leave one question the viewer needs answered. | The hook is explained right away (no gap left) |
| 10-20 s | Hard cut out of the hook (`[DROP]`), a time jump ("Twenty minutes ago..."), intro sting. | The sting comes before the hook, or runs longer than 6 s in a short video |
| 20-40 s | Prove the title: the viewer sees they are in the promised video; first small payoff. | Recap longer than one line; a new character without a want |

YouTube reports *Intro* in Studio's key moments as the share still watching at 30 s (**A**): that number is
the opening's grade. It also shows whether title and thumbnail promised what the first seconds deliver.

### Hook recipes for survival stories (pick one, write three, keep the best)

1. **Peak flash-forward** (default): the worst moment of THIS video, in present tense, mid-action.
   "The helicopter is spinning, and the whole city is spinning with it."
2. **You + number + constraint:** "You have four minutes to reach a roof three floors above the dead."
3. **Rule shock:** a world rule said flatly. "Every time a plane lands, every dead thing in the city walks to it."
4. **Impossible choice:** "One seat left on the last plane. Three of you."
5. **Sensory close-up:** one small physical detail before the wide danger. "Something warm is dripping. Fuel."

Rules for all of them: concrete nouns, no adjectives doing the work ("terrifying"), the danger must be visible
in the first shot, and the video must really reach that moment later (a fake hook kills trust, **C**).

### Cold open shape (10-20 s)

hook line -> it gets worse (one more beat, a sound) -> one human detail (a face, a hand) -> `[DROP]` hard cut
to black or silence -> the rewind line ("Twenty minutes ago, you were asleep.") -> sting.

### The intro sting

- Comes AFTER the cold open, never first. 6 s for 3-minute episodes, up to 18 s only in 20-minute episodes.
- Same every episode except the part number: recognition is the point (a series brand).
- Built from the strongest images of the series (hat, horde turning, plane, flare), on a sound that rises
  and slams. No narration over it.

### First payoff

Inside the first 40 s, answer something small (who is calling, why the stairs are blocked) while the big
question stays open. A viewer who got one answer trusts that the rest will come.

## Part 2: the ending

### The last 30 seconds

1. **Pay the episode's big loop** (the hook's question) 30-60 s before the end, so the ending is not spent on
   explanation.
2. **Cost:** something is lost or worse than at the start (an injury, a number down).
3. **Cliffhanger line** as the LAST spoken line. Types:
   - new threat in sight ("a thousand heads are turning towards you"),
   - reveal that changes the meaning of what we saw,
   - a choice put to the hero (and silently to the viewer),
   - callback twist (the cold open's object returns with a new meaning).
4. **End card** `PART N+1`, then 5 s of moving picture without voice for the end screen. End screens can
   only sit in the last 5-20 s (**A**); never put a story beat under them.

### Never do

- Summaries, "so that was...", "thanks for watching", long subscribe pleas: every curve drops at an outro
  (**C**). Closing ask under 8 s, and only after the cliffhanger has landed (**D**).
- A comment question before a payoff. Put it right after an emotional payoff, as one line that asks for a
  personal answer ("Would you leave her? Most people would.").
- Ending on calm. Even a warm ending carries one disturbing last image or line.

### Shorts and loops

For a vertical Short, write the last line so it flows into the first one (a replay counts as a view since
31 March 2025, **A**). For episodes, the "loop" is the next part: the last line is the question the next
title answers.

## Templates

**3-minute episode:** hook 0-3 s / cold open to ~0:15 / sting 6 s / set-up to 0:40 / set piece / pay the big
loop ~2:15-2:30 / cost / cliffhanger line / `PART N+1` + 5 s picture.

**20-minute episode:** hook 0-3 s / cold open to ~0:45 / sting up to 18 s / set-up to ~2:30 / ... / climax
reaches the cold-open moment and goes further / cliffhanger / end card + 5-10 s picture.

## Review checklist (score each 0-2, ship at 10+ of 12)

1. First spoken words contain danger, number or "you" (<= 3 s).
2. The hook's moment really happens later in the video.
3. One open question by 10 s; no explanation of it before the middle.
4. Sting placed after the cold open; length fits the format.
5. First small payoff by 40 s; recap is one line.
6. Last line is a cliffhanger; nothing after it except the end card and 5 s of picture.

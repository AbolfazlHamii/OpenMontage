# Audience retention: the middle of the video

Openings and endings are in `video-openings-endings`. This skill is everything in between, plus how to learn
from the numbers. Evidence grades as in `skills/creative/narration-craft.md` (**A** platform docs, **B**
research, **C** practice, **D** our thresholds).

## 1. The engine: a question always open

- **Loop ladder:** at any moment the viewer is waiting for 1-3 answers (never 0, never more than 3). Close one
  loop and open the next in the same breath ("but", "so", "then"): story beats are linked by *but* and
  *therefore*, never *and then* (**C**).
- **Big loop + small loops:** the episode's big question (will you make the plane?) stays open to the end or
  into the next part; small ones (who is on the speaker, what is dripping) open and close within 20-40 s.
- **Remind before you pay:** bring the question back just before its answer ("Remember the drawing?").
- **Countdown on screen** (planes left, km left, hours) is a loop the viewer reads without narration.

## 2. Re-hooks: something new every few seconds

| Format | New beat at least every | Shot length |
|---|---|---|
| Short / 3-minute episode | 5-8 s | 1.5-3 s, under 1 s at peaks |
| 20-minute episode | 8-12 s (**D**) | median 3-4 s (measured on the reference channel, **C**) |

A re-hook is a turn ("but"), a new number, a new sound, a new person, a rule stated, or a visual change of
scale (wide -> extreme close-up). Write them into the script; the edit lands its cuts on them.

## 3. Escalation

- Each obstacle is harder than the last, and each costs something that stays (injury, item, time).
- Intensity 1-5 per beat: never three 5s in a row, a 2 after every peak, the curve ends higher than it starts
  (`survival-episode-structure`).
- Inside a set piece: three obstacles, then a reversal (the plan fails, an improvised one works).
- Raise personal stakes, not only physical ones: the promise to Pip matters more than the horde size.

## 4. Pattern interrupts (picture and sound)

- **Picture:** change scale every 2-3 shots; insert close-ups of hands and objects; POV and through-glass
  shots; one held wide after every fast sequence so the viewer breathes; hard cuts, cuts to black on drops.
- **Sound:** silence before a reveal (`[BEAT]`, `[DROP]`), a sound that starts before its picture, a music
  drop at the turn, impacts on the cut. Silence is the strongest interrupt in a loud video.
- **Text:** typed cards for rules and countdowns only; never more than one card per 20 s in short episodes.

## 5. Cut dead time

Delete or shorten every line that: repeats what the picture shows, explains a feeling instead of showing it,
describes travel with no obstacle, lists without stakes, or recaps more than one line. Read each line and ask
"if this is gone, is anything lost?". In 3-minute episodes a 4-second dead line is 2% of the video.

## 6. Danger zones and fixes

| Where | Why viewers leave | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 0:15-0:40 | The sting or recap feels like waiting | Sting <= 6 s, recap one line, first payoff by 0:40 |
| Chapter ends | The story sounds finished | End every chapter on a new sign of trouble, never on rest |
| The middle (40-60%) | Same rhythm for too long | Midpoint turn: the plan breaks, a new rule, a quieter emotional scene |
| Before the end card | "It's over" signals | Last line is a cliffhanger; no outro |

## 7. Human beats keep people too

One warm beat per 60-90 s (a joke, a kindness, a small win): it gives the action a reason to matter and gives
the ear a rest (`human-story`). Short: one line, then straight back to danger.

## 8. Learning from the numbers (after publishing)

Use YouTube Studio > Analytics > Engagement for each video (**A**): *Intro* (share still watching at 30 s),
*Key moments for audience retention* (top moments, spikes, dips) and the average view duration.

1. Mark every dip > 3% on the script timeline: which line or shot was playing 2 s before it?
2. Mark spikes (rewatched moments): note what they share (action peaks, a rule card, a reveal).
3. Write one rule per finding into a `RETENTION_NOTES.md` in the project and apply it to the next episode.
4. Compare episodes only with the same format and similar age (first 7 days).

## Pre-publish checklist

1. Loop ladder drawn: 1-3 loops open at every point; every loop paid or carried to the next part.
2. Re-hook gap within the table for the format (mark the longest gap and fix it).
3. Intensity curve obeys the rules; a held wide after every fast sequence.
4. Every chapter ends on a new sign of trouble.
5. No line survives the "if this is gone" test without a reason.
6. A warm beat at least every 90 s.
7. Picture: no shot over 6 s in a short episode unless it is a deliberate breath.
